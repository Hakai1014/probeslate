Methodology

What our tools measure — and what they do not.

ProbeSlate treats browser diagnostics as engineering heuristics, not laboratory-certified instruments.

Microphone Buzz & Hum Test

The current microphone tool uses the Web Audio API and a 32,768-sample FFT. Frequency mapping uses the actual AudioContext sample rate. Frames are sampled with a best-effort 50% temporal overlap.

For candidate 50 Hz and 60 Hz harmonic families, the tool calculates tone prominence relative to nearby spectral background for each temporal frame. It summarizes persistence using the median and lower quartile (P25), and requires multiple persistent harmonically related components before classifying a mains-like family.

A single strong 50 Hz or 60 Hz tone is intentionally insufficient for a family classification.

Validation performed before first public deployment

  • Five-run same-session repeatability check under nominally unchanged conditions.
  • Controlled harmonic positive-control playback.
  • Single-transient / clap rejection test.
  • Microphone-session cleanup and rapid-click regression checks.

Important limitations

  • Browsers and operating systems may apply audio processing that the page cannot fully control.
  • Microphones capture acoustic pressure; fans, motors and HVAC can create patterns that resemble electrical mains-related hum.
  • A detected spectral pattern cannot prove a ground loop, charger fault, cable fault, USB fault or other physical cause.
  • The Before/After comparison is an observational troubleshooting aid, not formal statistical significance testing.
