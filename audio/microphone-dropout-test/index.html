    How to use the result

    This test inspects the microphone signal that reaches your browser. It looks for short stretches of digital silence surrounded by an active signal and isolated click-like spikes. Both are clues for troubleshooting, not proof that your microphone is faulty.

    If your microphone keeps cutting out

    Start with two runs under unchanged conditions. If an observation repeats, change one connection or setting and repeat the same passage. For a USB microphone, compare a hub with a direct port. For a headset, compare wired and Bluetooth modes if available. Note that switching connection type can also change the microphone, processing and audio format.

    If a problem happens only in Discord, a meeting app or a game, a clean browser run does not rule it out. Those applications may use different noise gates, input sensitivity settings or network paths. This page does not monitor their audio.

    If you hear crackling

    Check the click-like spike count alongside the conditions of the test. A keyboard tap, mouth click, contact with the microphone or another short sound can trigger the detector. Sustained crackling may not trigger this conservative detector. Repeat without touching the microphone before drawing a conclusion.

    If the result is inconclusive

    Speak clearly at a normal level and bring the microphone slightly closer if the signal is too quiet. If the input clips, lower its gain or move farther away. Keep the tab visible and rerun after a connection interruption. Do not increase playback volume to test the mic: this tool does not require speaker playback.

    For a continuous low buzz or hum, use the Microphone Buzz & Hum Test instead.

    What this test can measure

    This beta uses AudioWorklet to analyze incoming samples on the audio rendering thread. It inspects one input channel in approximately 1 ms windows for 25 seconds of received audio. It does not infer missing samples from delayed screen updates.

    • Suspected short dropout: a 4–250 ms near-zero segment with at least 98% effectively zero samples, surrounded by 20 ms flanks whose median RMS exceeds −50.5 dBFS. Boundaries are approximate to one analysis window. This pattern can also come from processing or muting.
    • Click-like spike: an isolated sample excursion exceeding 0.08 full scale and 12 times the preceding window RMS, with opposite slopes and close neighbouring samples. Events within 10 ms are grouped. This does not cover every form of crackle.
    • Near-zero segment: at least 4 ms with window RMS below −80 dBFS. These are reported separately and never treated as faults on their own.
    • Input state events: mute, unmute and ended notifications from the selected track. A source can hide interruptions without emitting these events.
    • Test quality: at least 20% of windows must exceed −50.5 dBFS. Excessive clipping, disrupted timing or input events make a run inconclusive. An active fan can satisfy the level check: it is not a speech detector.

    Browser and operating-system processing may hide, alter or create these patterns. Audio rendering time is not a direct measurement of USB packet timing, Bluetooth packet loss or driver performance. No continuity percentage or hardware-health score is assigned.

    Before / After is observational. A retained connection provides a closer comparison, but changing devices requires a new connection; the result identifies this. Different speaking levels and unobserved processing remain confounders.

    Audio samples are processed transiently in memory, never uploaded or saved as a recording. Only measurement summaries remain in this page until you clear them or leave. Ending a session releases the microphone; hiding the page cancels an active test and releases it. Privacy details.
